What size screws for decking?

Use a screw 4.5 to 5 mm in diameter and at least two and a half times as long as the board is thick. For a 28 mm deck board that means 65 to 70 mm.

The aim is to get about two thirds of the screw into the joist. A screw that is too short holds for a season, then works loose as the timber swells and shrinks.

How many screws

  • Two screws through each board at every joist.
  • Set each screw about a quarter of the board width in from the edge, and never closer than 15 mm.
  • With joists at 400 mm centres, allow 35 to 40 screws per square metre of deck.

Stop the boards splitting

Drill a pilot hole for any screw near the end of a board. In hardwood, pilot and countersink every screw. In softwood, a sharp screw will usually start itself away from the ends.

Coating matters more than size

Ordinary bright or yellow zinc screws are for indoor use. Outdoors they rust, and the preservative in treated timber speeds that up. For decking, use screws with an exterior coating or stainless steel. Stainless is the safer choice near the coast and in oak or other hardwoods, where a rusting screw leaves black stains.

The frame underneath

Joists and bearers need longer, heavier fixings than the boards. Long wafer head timber screws pull a frame together without washers. Coach screws suit bolting joists and bearers to posts.

Common questions

Can I use chipboard screws outside?

Only if they have an exterior coating. Indoor zinc plated chipboard screws will rust in a deck, usually within a couple of winters.

Do I need to pre-drill decking?

At board ends, yes, in any timber. In hardwood, pre-drill every screw. In softwood away from the ends, most decking screws drive without a pilot hole.

Screws or nails for decking?

Screws. They hold better as boards move with the weather, and a board can be lifted later to reach what is underneath.
