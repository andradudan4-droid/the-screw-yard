Which screw for plasterboard?

Into timber, use a coarse thread drywall screw: 38 mm for 12.5 mm board. Into metal stud, use a fine thread drywall screw: 25 mm for 12.5 mm board.

Screw length by board thickness

The screw should go at least 25 mm into timber, or at least 10 mm through a metal stud. That gives these lengths:

Coarse or fine thread

Coarse thread has a wide, deep thread that grips softwood studs, joists and battens without stripping. Fine thread has two shallow threads close together. It bites into thin steel where a coarse thread would chew the hole out and spin.

Both have a bugle head. The curved underside presses the paper face down without tearing it, which is why an ordinary countersunk wood screw is the wrong choice for plasterboard.

Heavier steel

A fine thread screw only pierces light gauge stud. For steel from about 2 mm thick, such as structural framing or heavy channel, use a bugle head self-drilling screw. Its drill point cuts the hole before the thread engages.

Spacing and depth

  • Walls: a screw every 300 mm along each stud.
  • Ceilings: a screw every 230 mm along each joist.
  • Keep at least 10 mm in from the paper-bound long edges and 13 mm from cut ends.
  • Drive the head just below the surface. If it breaks through the paper it is holding very little, so put another screw beside it.

Finish

Black or grey phosphate is the normal finish for dry indoor work, and filler keys to it well. In kitchens, bathrooms and other damp areas, choose a galvanised finish.

Boarding a lot?

Collated drywall screws come on a plastic strip for auto-feed screw guns. On a full house they save a good deal of time, and the gun sets every head to the same depth.

Common questions

Can I use wood screws for plasterboard?

It is not a good idea. A countersunk wood screw has a sharp cone under the head that cuts the paper face, and the paper is what gives the board its strength. A drywall screw has a bugle head made to seat without tearing.

How many screws does one sheet need?

A 2400 x 1200 mm sheet on a wall with studs at 400 mm centres takes about 36 screws. With studs at 600 mm centres it takes about 27. Ceilings need more because the spacing is closer.

Do drywall screws rust?

Phosphate screws are for dry indoor use and will rust if they stay damp. Use a galvanised finish anywhere moisture is likely.
