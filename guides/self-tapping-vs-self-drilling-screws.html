Self-tapping or self-drilling screws: which do you need?

A self-tapping screw cuts its own thread but needs a pilot hole in metal. A self-drilling screw has a drill point, so it makes the hole and the thread in one go.

When self-tapping is the right choice

Thin sheet is too light for a drill point: the point punches through before the thread has anything to bite. A self-tapping screw in a small pilot hole gives a tighter fixing. They also work well in plastics and for fixing metal brackets to timber.

When self-drilling is the right choice

On steel from about 2 mm upwards, drilling a pilot hole for every fixing is slow. A self-drilling screw does it in one pass. The rule is that the drill point must be longer than the steel is thick, so the hole is finished before the thread starts. That is why thicker steel needs the longer point found on hex head screws.

Timber to steel

Use a self-drilling screw with wings. The wings ream a clearance hole through the timber so the thread does not jack the board away from the steel, then snap off when they reach the metal.

Roofing and cladding

  • Sheets to steel purlins: hex head self-drilling screws with a bonded sealing washer.
  • Sheets to timber: roofing screws with a sharp point and a washer.
  • Tighten until the washer is just compressed. If the rubber squeezes out sideways it is overtightened and will leak.

Driving them

Use a moderate drill speed and steady pressure on thick steel. Too fast and the point overheats and goes blunt before it is through.

Common questions

Are Tek screws the same as self-drilling screws?

Yes. Tek is a brand name that has become the trade word for self-drilling screws.

Do self-tapping screws need a pilot hole?

In metal, yes. In thin soft sheet, plastics and timber, a sharp point will often start without one.

Can I use self-drilling screws in wood?

They will hold, but the drill point does nothing useful in timber. Use them where timber is being fixed to steel, ideally the winged type.
