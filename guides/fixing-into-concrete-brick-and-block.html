How to fix into concrete, brick and block

Light loads: a plug and screw. Medium loads: a concrete screw, straight into a drilled hole. Heavy loads in solid concrete: a wedge anchor.

Concrete screws

A 7.5 mm concrete screw goes into a 6 mm hole. The thread cuts into the wall of the hole, so there is no plug and no expansion. That makes them a good choice near an edge, where an expanding anchor could crack the concrete.

Wedge and drop-in anchors

Both grip by expanding against the hole. They need solid concrete around them, so keep them away from edges and do not use them in brick, block or mortar joints. A drop-in anchor is set with a setting tool and leaves an internal thread flush with the surface, ready for threaded bar.

Getting a hole that holds

  • Use the drill size marked for the fixing. A worn or oversize bit is the usual reason a fixing spins.
  • Drill at least 10 mm deeper than the fixing goes in, so dust has somewhere to sit.
  • Blow or brush the dust out. A dusty hole can halve the grip.
  • Fix into the brick or block itself, not the mortar joint.

This is general guidance. For structural or safety-critical fixings, follow the anchor maker's load data or an engineer's specification.

Common questions

Can I use wedge anchors in brick?

No. Wedge anchors are made for solid concrete. In brick they can split the brick or pull out. Use a concrete screw or a frame anchor instead.

What size hole for a concrete screw?

A 7.5 mm concrete screw needs a 6 mm hole, drilled at least 10 mm deeper than the screw will go.

What is the best fixing close to an edge?

A concrete screw. It does not expand, so it puts much less stress on the concrete than a wedge or shield anchor.
