Screw sizes explained: gauge and length

A metric screw size is diameter x length in millimetres. A 5 x 80 screw is 5 mm across the thread and 80 mm long.

Gauge to millimetres

Older screws and many plans still use gauge numbers. These are the nearest metric sizes:

So the old "8 by 2 inch" is a 4 x 50 today, and a "10 by 3 inch" is a 5 x 80.

How length is measured

Length is the part that ends up in the material. A countersunk screw is measured over its whole length, head included, because the head sits flush. A pan head or hex head screw is measured from under the head.

Choosing the length

About two thirds of the screw should be in the piece you are fixing into. A quick rule: pick a screw two and a half to three times as long as the piece on top is thick. An 18 mm board onto a frame takes a 45 to 50 mm screw.

Bolt sizes

Bolts and machine screws use M sizes. M8 means a thread 8 mm in diameter. The spanner size is different: 10 mm for M6, 13 mm for M8, 17 mm for most M10 and 19 mm for most M12.

Common questions

What is an 8 gauge screw in mm?

About 4.0 mm in diameter. A 10 gauge is about 5.0 mm and a 6 gauge about 3.5 mm.

What does 5 x 80 mean on a box of screws?

The screw is 5 mm in diameter and 80 mm long.

Is a 4 mm screw the same as an 8 gauge?

For practical purposes, yes. Metric 4.0 mm replaced 8 gauge and they use the same plugs and pilot holes.
